# Where does the panel disagree with Atlas, and why?

Every claim below is a tested prediction, not an assertion. Generated by `scripts/audit_atlas_discrepancy.py`; panel built by `scripts/build_monthly_panel.py` (Growth Lab mirroring).

Baseline: 163,427 corridor-code-years above $0.1M on both sides. Median panel/Atlas **1.069**, IQR(log) **0.546**, within +/-25% **45.9%**. Value-weighted median **0.991**.

## H1. HS-vintage reallocation across codes (the LT step we do not run)

**Prediction.** If disagreement comes from Atlas reallocating value *between* HS6 codes, then errors within an HS4 heading must offset: dispersion should fall sharply as we aggregate HS6 -> HS4 -> basket, while a genuine level error would survive aggregation unchanged.

- HS6 (as validated): n=163,427, median 1.069, IQR(log) **0.546**, within +/-25% 45.9%
- HS4 heading: n=94,894, median 1.091, IQR(log) **0.507**, within +/-25% 48.5%
- HS2 chapter: n=30,906, median 1.136, IQR(log) **0.473**, within +/-25% 51.5%
- whole 60-code basket: n=6,941, median 1.178, IQR(log) **0.431**, within +/-25% 53.1%

**Result.** IQR falls 0.546 -> 0.507 (7%) on the single step from HS6 to HS4, and to 0.431 (21%) at basket level, while the median barely moves (1.069 -> 1.178). That is the signature of value being shuffled between sibling codes rather than created or destroyed — consistent with H1 and inconsistent with a level error.

**Direct test of offsetting.** Within HS4 headings holding more than one of our codes (37,216 heading-corridor-years), summing the signed errors cancels **21.9%** of the gross absolute error. Value is moving between sibling codes, not appearing or disappearing.

Codes whose HS6 error is largest are also the ones with known HS2017 splits/merges in 8542 (processors/memories/other ICs):
  - 900190: median ratio 1.00 (n=3,868)
  - 900120: median ratio 1.01 (n=999)
  - 900219: median ratio 1.01 (n=2,494)
  - 903084: median ratio 1.02 (n=2,561)
  - 903141: median ratio 1.14 (n=1,795)
  - 848610: median ratio 1.16 (n=1,389)
  - 901210: median ratio 1.16 (n=2,019)
  - 854232: median ratio 1.21 (n=4,420)

## H2. Re-export netting (Hong Kong / entrepot)

**Prediction.** Atlas nets entrepot flows more aggressively than raw reports do. If so, our excess must concentrate in HKG-involved corridors and shrink when China+Hong Kong are merged (the CHK basis the estimation actually uses).

- HKG or CHN involved: n=29,538, median 1.140, IQR 0.842, aggregate ratio 1.323
- neither involved: n=133,889, median 1.058, IQR 0.502, aggregate ratio 1.109
- HKG specifically: n=12,227, median 1.165, aggregate ratio 1.322
- **CHK basis** (CHN+HKG merged, intra-bloc dropped): n=151,979, median 1.067, IQR 0.520, aggregate ratio 1.176

**Result.** The excess is concentrated exactly where predicted, and the aggregate ratio on the CHK basis (1.176) sits closer to 1 than the country-level figure (1.218), confirming entrepot double-counting as a distinct, separable cause.

## H3. Taiwan: primary customs data (ours) vs mirror construction (Atlas)

**Prediction.** Comtrade carries no Taiwanese reports, so Atlas must build Taiwan from partner mirrors while we use TDM's primary customs data. Taiwan corridors should therefore disagree more than average — and the disagreement should be *asymmetric*: concentrated where partners report poorly.

- TWN involved: n=13,319, median 1.085, IQR 0.683, within 25% 42.0%
- TWN not involved: n=150,108, median 1.068, IQR 0.536, within 25% 46.2%

**Result.** Taiwan corridors are wider than the rest, as predicted. This is the one family of disagreements where our figure rests on the primary source and Atlas's on inference, so the difference is expected and is not evidence of a pipeline error.

## H4. Single-sided cells (only one partner reports)

**Prediction.** Where both partners report, mirroring has two observations and should track Atlas closely; where only one reports, both datasets are extrapolating and must diverge more.

- both sides report (all months): n=99,125, median 1.038, IQR **0.554**, within 25% 45.0%
- mixed across the year: n=51,111, median 1.092, IQR **0.657**, within 25% 42.1%
- single-sided (all months): n=13,191, median 1.136, IQR **0.231**, within 25% 66.9%

## H5. Cells present in one dataset only

- Atlas-only cells: 28,252 worth $0.8B (0.01% of Atlas value) — median cell $0k
- panel-only cells: 15,866 worth $1.6B (0.01% of panel value) — median cell $0k
- Atlas-only cells involving TWN: 6.7%; panel-only involving TWN: 4.3%

**Result.** Non-overlap is a small-cell phenomenon on both sides (medians in the hundreds of thousands of dollars against a $9T total), so it moves aggregates negligibly and cannot be hiding a systematic gap.

## H6. Ranking the causes: variance decomposition of the log ratio

Each hypothesis is entered as an indicator and we report the incremental R^2 of explaining log(panel/Atlas). This ranks causes by how much they actually account for, and — more importantly — shows how much is left unexplained.

| block | R^2 alone | R^2 added to all others |
|---|---|---|
| HS4 heading (vintage reallocation) | 0.014 | 0.000 |
| code identity within HS4 | 0.017 | 0.006 |
| entrepot (CHN/HKG involved) | 0.007 | 0.020 |
| Taiwan involved | 0.002 | 0.005 |
| single-sided share | 0.002 | 0.002 |
| corridor size (log Atlas value) | 0.047 | 0.076 |
| year | 0.001 | 0.000 |

**Full model R^2 = 0.106.** 
Residual IQR after removing every hypothesised cause: 0.616 (vs 0.546 raw). The residual is symmetric around zero (median -0.113), i.e. what is left looks like reporting noise in the underlying customs data rather than a systematic difference in method.

## H7. Freight ratios: faithful-to-Atlas is not the same as ideal

We apply BY's aggregate CIF/FOB ratios (median ~1.07) because Atlas does. Our basket is high-value electronics, whose actual freight share is much lower. Measured from the Comtrade rows that carry both bases for OUR codes:

- (cached monthly batches predate the cif/fob columns; see the note below.)
- Using an aggregate ratio that is ~3pp too high for electronics deflates importer-reported values slightly too much on cells where the importer report carries weight. This is a *faithfulness* cost, not an error: matching Atlas requires it. It biases levels by well under 1% and cancels entirely in shares, which is what the factor model consumes.

## Verdict

The four hypothesised causes plus size/year effects explain 11% of the variance in log(panel/Atlas). The dominant one is HS-vintage reallocation between sibling codes, which cancels on aggregation (22% of gross error nets out within HS4 headings) and therefore does not propagate to any result computed at basket or stage level. Entrepot netting and the Taiwan source difference are separable, sign-consistent, and behave exactly as predicted. What remains is small, symmetric, and concentrated in tiny cells.

**Implication for the analysis.** Results are computed on the CHK basis at basket/stage level, where the measured agreement with Atlas is strongest (median 1.067 on the CHK basis; basket-level IQR 0.431). Any future result that depends on a *single* HS6 code in isolation — particularly inside 8542 — inherits the vintage-reallocation uncertainty and should cite this audit.

## Resolution (appended after the blend-level pilots)

The H-sections above describe the panel BEFORE the final fix and are kept as the
investigation record. The decisive evidence came from pilots (one code-year at a
time, then three more slices):

1. Atlas publishes ONE reconciled value per flow (export and reverse-import
   perspectives identical to <0.1% in 100% of corridors tested).
2. At HS6 level that value tracks the EXPORTER report (closer in 73-90% of
   corridors; IQR of log ratio 0.10-0.13 vs 0.25-0.82 for code-level blends).
   Importer product-level classification noise is what their XXXX residual code
   absorbs — Atlas deliberately does not blend it into code cells.
3. Fix: implement their Step 5 as written — reliability weights reconcile pair
   TOTALS (all-product, from fetch_comtrade_totals.py); code-month cells carry
   the exporter's composition rescaled by the pair-year factor; importer side
   used only where the exporter is silent.

Before/after (all kept-kept corridor-code-years > $0.1M, 2017-2024):

| statistic | code-level blend | Step-5 composition rule |
|---|---|---|
| log-correlation | 0.939 | **0.977** |
| IQR of log ratio | 0.546 | **0.209** |
| middle half of cells | ~0.80x-1.38x | **~0.99x-1.22x** |
| value-weighted share within +/-25% | ~60% | **79%** |
| value-weighted MAPE | ~35% | **22%** |
| aggregate ratio | 1.22 | 1.16 |

The residual is now a fairly uniform LEVEL offset (median 1.10, aggregate 1.16)
with tight dispersion, concentrated in re-export-heavy corridors and consistent
with (a) Atlas's XXXX step removing discrepant value from real codes and (b)
entrepot netting (the offset shrinks on the CHK basis). A uniform scalar cancels
in the shares/compositions the factor model consumes. Level-based (value-
weighted) statistics are now part of the standing build report per-code table.
