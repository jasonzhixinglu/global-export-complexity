# Findings — concentration & fragmentation statistics (2017-12 .. 2026-04, audited panel)

Interpretation of the series in this folder. Measures defined in
docs/concentration-fragmentation-nnf-mfm-trade.md; generated by
`scripts/network_stats.py` on the 60-code panel (docs/data.md §3) and the
re-estimated TV-MFM. Hand-written — not regenerated by the script.

**Revision note.** An earlier version of this file (git history) reported the
alignment covariance flipping from negative to positive at the 2023-07 break as
the headline finding. That result **does not replicate** on the audited panel —
it was an artifact of the pre-audit reconciliation, which blended importer-side
classification noise into code cells. Findings below are from the corrected
panel; where an old finding survived, that is stated explicitly.

## 1. Alignment covariance: no compounding-concentration signature in the AI era (revised)

On the corrected panel the AI-compute alignment covariance is mildly positive
from 2017, peaks around the **first tariff war** (0.026 in 2019), and decays
toward zero through the AI boom (0.008 by 2024, ~0 by 2026). The H100 shock
reorganized *which* countries anchor the hubs (the 2023-08 loading break is the
dominant one in the TV-MFM), but it did **not** make tight export hubs link
disproportionately to tight import hubs — demand concentration and supply
concentration rose without compounding at the channel level.

![AI compute — joint concentration decomposition on the audited panel; the alignment covariance panel peaks around 2019 and decays through the AI era](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jasonzhixinglu/global-export-complexity/main/results/network_stats/ai_compute/decomposition.png)

By code: servers carry a persistently positive alignment (0.04–0.08 through
2025 — the one segment where concentrated supply does serve concentrated
demand) that collapses in 2026 as assembly origins diversify; parts sit at zero
essentially throughout, consistent with their 91-month no-break run.

## 2. Bloc fragmentation is robust — and now visibly TWO-PHASE (survived, extended)

The cross-bloc CN↔US channel share of `g` falls ~70% over the sample
(0.117 in 2017 → 0.034 in 2026), and the longer window resolves it into two
distinct leg-downs separated by a plateau:

- **Leg 1, 2019** (first tariff war): 0.119 → 0.082, with servers the sharpest
  (0.085 → 0.044, halving in one year).
- **Plateau 2020–22**: no recovery, no further decline (0.070–0.074).
- **Leg 2, 2023 onward** (export controls + AI reorganization + 2025 tariffs):
  0.073 → 0.034, while within-US-bloc rises 0.135 → 0.232.

![AI compute — two notions of fragmentation on the audited panel; the cross-bloc share (red, right panel) steps down in 2019, plateaus, then declines again from 2023](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jasonzhixinglu/global-export-complexity/main/results/network_stats/ai_compute/fragmentation.png)

Policy events cut trade links fast and the links do not regrow between events —
fragmentation is a ratchet, not a cycle. By code:

- **847180 (baseboards)**: the extreme case — cross-bloc peaks in 2021 (0.109)
  then collapses ~20x to 0.005 by 2026, while linkage concentration HHI_F rises
  0.09 → 0.48: baseboard trade is becoming one channel, inside one bloc. The
  sharpest decoupling, in the code most exposed to export controls (survived,
  sharper than before).
- **847150 (servers)**: within-US-bloc share doubles to 0.40 by 2026; cross-bloc
  down to 0.021 (survived).
- **847330 (parts)**: cross-bloc halves but remains the HIGHEST of the three
  codes throughout (0.153 → 0.076) — the parts layer is still the
  globally-shared substrate (survived).

Within-CN-bloc shares stay tiny everywhere (<0.01): the "two parallel circuits"
picture remains one thickening US-bloc circuit plus a China spoke (survived).

## 3. Hub-overlap fragmentation: still mainly a diagnostic (unchanged caveat)

Under the anchor-identified basis hubs are near-disjoint by construction, so
overlap fragmentation is range-compressed (mostly 0.87–0.99). Its one large
move — a dip to 0.67 in 2024 — coincides with the transition between the long
pre-AI era and the post-2023-08 regime and with elevated clipped negative mass,
so it reads as basis stress during regime change, not as an economic overlap
event. The bloc measures above, which do not depend on hub disjointness, remain
the fragmentation series to quote.

![AI compute — per-hub linkage concentration and the clipped-negative-mass basis diagnostic on the audited panel](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/jasonzhixinglu/global-export-complexity/main/results/network_stats/ai_compute/linkage.png)

## 4. Concentration vs market power: replicated on the corrected panel (survived, sharper)

The dependence-vs-power asymmetry not only survived the audit — the numbers
sharpened:

| relationship | own dest HHI (dependence) | counterparty's origin HHI (power) | outcome under stress |
|---|---|---|---|
| MEX → USA, servers 847150 | 0.82 (2023 and 2025, unchanged) | US origin HHI 0.41 → 0.43 (flat) | corridor contested within a quarter — no power |
| TWN → USA, baseboards 847180 | 0.30 → 0.47 | US origin HHI 0.42 → **0.83** | 20x unit-value repricing — power |

Your own concentration on a counterparty measures dependence; what tracks
pricing power is the counterparty's concentration on you. Mexico's total
dependence on the US buyer (0.82) never translated into leverage because the US
was never concentrated on Mexico; the US *did* become concentrated on Taiwan in
exactly the code where the repricing happened, and more so on the corrected
panel (0.83 vs 0.79 before). Upstream raw-material stages can now be tested the
same way — the panel carries stages 1–5 monthly from 2017 — and that extension
is the natural next use of this module.

## Caveats

- Hub-level series are computed from the reconstructed low-rank flows
  (R² 0.93–0.99); the power tables in the README are raw-panel.
- Loadings are clipped to the simplex; clipped mass is tracked per month in
  `series.csv` and spikes during the 2023–24 regime transition.
- The panel-vs-Atlas residual is a near-uniform level scalar (docs/data.md §3);
  it cancels in every share-based measure here.
